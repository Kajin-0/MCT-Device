## Harman 1980 direct operating-scale state

Direct primary values:

- Te-rich slider under flowing H2;
- growth `450–550 °C`;
- contact/growth time `0.25–10 min`;
- typical equilibration `~1 h at 550 °C`;
- source wafers + supercooled solutions + `(111)` substrates used for highest-quality layers;
- layer thicknesses `3–15 µm`.

These are not one fixed recipe and are not to be averaged with the Honeywell ~30-min patent example.

## Source-preparation branch separation

Bernardi 1988 demonstrates a separate method:

- in-situ preparation of Te-rich MCT solution via Hg vapor transport into Cd-rich Te melt;
- avoids separate ampoule solution preparation/homogenization for each run.

This is **not** merged with sealed-ampoule synthesis.

## Thermal trajectory evidence

Shinohara 1994 directly shows that thermal history materially changes thickness:

- equilibrium cooling -> `2–4 µm` thin layers;
- step/supercool branch with `15 K` supercooling -> `30–40 µm` layers;
- composition range `x=.25–.42`.

Therefore P30 records full `T(t)`, actual supercooling at contact and cooling rate; `Tgrowth` alone is insufficient.

## Honeywell wipe-off reconstruction

### US4592304A

Direct:

- dedicated wipe-off well;
- several CdTe pieces in vertical slots about `1 mm` apart;
- pieces can be polycrystalline/unpolished;
- mechanical wipe + surface-tension adhesion + capillary wicking;
- discard pieces after cooldown.

### US4706604A

Later separate hardware generation:

- CdTe apron in tandem with substrate;
- diagonal diamond scribes;
- growth solution overlaps apron;
- residual film/drop is directed onto apron and prevented from migrating back;
- finite slider clearance is necessary to avoid epilayer scratches;
- patent reports complete/100% wipe-off in this arrangement.

These two generations remain separate recipe branches.

## New permanent P30 rules

1. `xL/yL` defines composition, not total charge mass.
2. Growth time from different apparatus/thermal branches must not be averaged.
3. Sealed-ampoule and in-situ solution preparation are separate process families.
4. Wipe-off hardware generations are separate process families.
5. Slider clearance/speed/smoothness are material-process variables.
6. Thermal trajectory must be recorded as `T(t)`.
7. Hg-source mass, geometry and reuse form a genealogy that must accompany run-order composition data.
8. Melt/source reuse observations are repeated measures, not independent process runs.
