# P15 research record — cryogenic package / die attach / interconnect

**Date:** 2026-08-15 America/New_York

## Historical RP-01 finding

The audited Smith et al. 2001 process/performance paper does not disclose the mechanical package construction used for its 80-K measurements.

Not recovered from RP-01:

- die singulation;
- die outline;
- die attach;
- cold finger/carrier;
- wire material/diameter/bond technique;
- package/header;
- window/aperture/cold shield;
- vacuum level;
- bakeout;
- cryogenic-cycle qualification.

Therefore no such parameter is to be backfilled as historical fact.

## Broad detector architecture evidence

Independent experimental HgCdTe photoconductor work uses cryogenic elements encapsulated/mounted on a cold finger in a Dewar cooled near 77 K. This supports the broad package architecture only; it does not close RP-01 materials or geometry.

## Same-UWA package thermal-history caution

UWA work by Nguyen, Musca, Dell and Faraone, “The effects of vacuum baking on the I-V characteristics of LWIR HgCdTe photodiodes” (SPIE 5274, 2004), establishes a relevant caution: thermal/vacuum package history can alter HgCdTe device electrical behavior.

Because the source concerns LWIR photodiodes, do not transfer its numerical bake conditions into RP-01. Use it to enforce the rule that package bakeout must stay inside a detector-qualified thermal budget.

## Mechanical-test framework

Current DLA ASSIST records show MIL-STD-883 Part 2 remains the active mechanical-test-method section for microelectronic devices.

Relevant method classes after construction is fixed:

- Method 2011 — destructive bond strength / bond pull;
- Method 2019 — die shear.

The project must record exact standard revision/test condition when those methods are used. Numerical limits cannot be selected until wire and die-attach construction are known.

## Core P15 decision

Packaging is not qualified merely because the die remains attached and wires remain intact.

The package process is accepted only if it preserves the detector state established upstream.

Required pre/post comparisons include, as appropriate:

- I-V / resistance;
- contact/lead resistance;
- P12 noise ASD and 1/f behavior;
- P11 responsivity/throughput;
- P13 bandwidth/time response where package parasitics matter;
- detector temperature under P10 bias;
- optical FOV/throughput.

## Die-attach selection principle

Historical material unknown.

Do not assume conductive epoxy, silver epoxy, solder/eutectic or clamp.

Choose by a local DOE measuring:

- bondline/voiding;
- thermal resistance;
- die stress/tilt;
- thermal-cycle survival;
- electrical/noise changes;
- package vacuum compatibility.

## Wire-bond selection principle

The device has an Au top metal, but this does not prove historical Au wire or any specific bond process.

Freeze wire material/diameter and bonding mode only after coupon qualification of force/ultrasonic/time/stage-temperature/process-window and destructive pull/failure-mode data.

## Optical package principle

P11 makes package geometry part of the radiometric chain.

Record actual:

- window/filter transmission;
- aperture dimensions;
- detector/aperture distances;
- cold-shield geometry;
- vignetting;
- geometric FOV.

Do not simply label a package “60° FOV.” The earlier Planck consistency result supports a provisional full-angle interpretation for the historical RP-01 label but does not define the physical package.
