# Same-UWA anodic-oxide source recovery

**Date:** 2026-08-15 America/New_York

## Question

Can the exact native-anodic-oxide process used in the UWA/Smith/Musca/Faraone HgCdTe photoconductor lineage be recovered strongly enough to replace P02's current historical transfer candidate?

## Direct RP-01 evidence

Smith et al. 2001 directly states that the experimental photoconductive devices used approximately:

- native anodic oxide thickness: `800 Å = 80 nm`.

The currently audited RP-01 paper does not state:

- electrolyte composition;
- solvent ratio;
- anodization current density;
- anode/cathode fixture;
- bath temperature;
- endpoint voltage;
- anodization time;
- rinse/dry sequence;
- oxide thickness calibration method.

Therefore only the **film identity/thickness** is direct RP-01 process evidence.

## Same-UWA source recovered

C. Musca, E. P. G. Smith, J. Dell, L. Faraone, “Performance and stability of HgCdTe photoconductive devices: A study of contact and passivation technology,” in *1998 Conference on Optoelectronic and Microelectronic Materials and Devices Proceedings*, IEEE, published 1999, pp. 283–286, ISBN 0-7803-4513-4.

This source is extremely relevant because:

- it is explicitly about HgCdTe photoconductive devices;
- it focuses on contact and passivation technology;
- Musca, Smith, Dell and Faraone directly overlap the RP-01 team;
- it predates RP-01 by roughly two years and therefore likely represents immediate process lineage.

### Current limitation

The publicly accessible UWA repository record exposes bibliographic metadata but not the experimental section/process recipe. No electrolyte/current-density/endpoint/time information was recoverable from the indexed record.

Status:

- same-UWA passivation/contact paper identity = `CLOSED-P-BIBLIOGRAPHIC`;
- exact anodization recipe from that paper = `OPEN`.

## Earlier UWA photoconductor passivation lineage

C. A. Musca, J. F. Siliquini, B. Nener, L. Faraone, “Passivation and Surface Effects in Long Wavelength Infrared HgCdTe Photoconductors,” SPIE Proceedings Vol. 2552, pp. 158–169 (1995).

This is additional direct UWA photoconductor-passivation lineage. The publicly indexed repository record again provides bibliographic metadata but does not expose a laboratory anodization recipe.

A related UWA photoconductor article:

J. F. Siliquini, L. Faraone, “A Gate-Controlled HgCdTe Long Wavelength Infrared Photoconductive Detector,” *Semiconductor Science and Technology* 12, 1010–1015 (1997), DOI `10.1088/0268-1242/12/8/014`.

This paper confirms the physical importance of native-oxide/ZnS surface conditions in HgCdTe photoconductors, but it does not presently close RP-01's native-oxide formation conditions.

## UWA ZnS/hydrogenation branch — not RP-01 oxide recipe

J. K. White, C. Musca, H. C. Lee, L. Faraone, “Hydrogenation of ZnS passivation on narrow-band gap HgCdTe,” *Applied Physics Letters* 76, 2448–2450 (2000), DOI `10.1063/1.126372`, is useful same-laboratory evidence that CH4/H2 plasma processing can modify passivant/interface behavior.

Do not interpret it as evidence that RP-01 used ZnS on the experimental devices. RP-01 explicitly identifies 800-Å anodic oxide for its experimental devices; adding ZnS requires separate evidence.

## Primary non-UWA chemistry context

### Nemirovsky/Finkman anodic-oxide lineage

Older primary HgCdTe work reports anodic native-oxide formation using KOH-based electrolytes and identifies oxide chemistry/interface behavior. These sources establish that native anodization of HgCdTe was a mature process family before the UWA work, but they do not prove which exact recipe UWA selected.

### Texas Instruments 800-Å transfer candidate

U.S. Patent 3,977,018 remains the strongest detailed historical process candidate presently available for producing approximately the RP-01 film thickness:

- `0.1 M KOH`;
- `90% ethylene glycol / 10% deionized water`;
- constant current around `0.3 mA/cm²`;
- endpoint near `15 V`;
- approximately `2 min`;
- approximately `800 Å` oxide.

This candidate is independently consistent with later HgCdTe experimental literature using the same electrolyte/current-density family.

## Critical conclusion

The new UWA source recovery **does not justify promoting the TI recipe into a direct RP-01 recipe**.

Current evidence hierarchy remains:

1. RP-01 directly closes native anodic oxide and `80 nm` thickness.
2. Same-UWA Musca/Smith/Dell/Faraone 1999 paper proves that passivation/contact technology was actively studied in the exact RP-01 laboratory lineage, but its recipe is not publicly recovered.
3. Older/later independent HgCdTe sources strongly support the `0.1 M KOH / EG-water / constant-current` family as a credible transfer candidate.
4. Exact UWA electrolyte/current/endpoint/time remains `[OPEN]`.

P02 must therefore remain a **qualification candidate**, not a historical exact-recipe claim.
