## S-R7-01 — Siliquini et al. 1994 — full-surface/sidewall passivation importance

**Class:** Primary-A / Same-UWA-lineage  
**Citation:** J. F. Siliquini, K. A. Fynn, B. D. Nener, L. Faraone, R. H. Hartley, “Improved device technology for epitaxial Hg1−xCdxTe infrared photoconductor arrays,” *Semiconductor Science and Technology* 9, 1515–1522 (1994).  
**DOI:** `10.1088/0268-1242/9/8/013`

**Key result:** in the LWIR epitaxial photoconductors studied, leaving mesa/device sidewalls unpassivated can reduce responsivity by roughly a factor of two.

**Role:** same-lab evidence that sidewall surface recombination is an electrically important process variable.

**Restriction:** x/geometry differ from RP-01. The factor-of-two value is not an RP-01 acceptance target.

**Procedure:** `procedures/P02C_MESA_SIDEWALL_PASSIVATION_QUALIFICATION.md`.

## S-R7-03 — Siliquini & Faraone 1996 — 3×3 n+ blocking-contact arrays

**Class:** Primary-A / Same-UWA-lineage  
**Citation:** J. F. Siliquini, L. Faraone, “Two-Dimensional Infrared Focal Plane Arrays Based on HgCdTe Photoconductive Detectors,” *Semiconductor Science and Technology* 11, 1906–1911 (1996).  
**DOI:** `10.1088/0268-1242/11/12/024`

**Key result:** 3×3 n-type epitaxial HgCdTe photoconductor arrays with n+ blocking contacts; all elements demonstrated background-limited performance at 80 K.

**Restriction:** accessible abstract does not expose how the n+ regions were fabricated. Do not infer that they were RIE-formed.

## S-R7-04 — Siliquini et al. 1994 — optimized blocking/overlap photoconductors

**Class:** Primary-A / Same-UWA functional lineage  
**Citation:** J. F. Siliquini, C. A. Musca, B. D. Nener, L. Faraone, “Performance of optimized Hg1−xCdxTe long wavelength infrared photoconductors,” *Infrared Physics & Technology* 35(5), 661–671 (1994).  
**DOI:** `10.1016/1350-4495(94)90059-0`

**Key result:** combined overlap geometry and blocking-contact structures were used to reduce minority-carrier sweepout; improved responsivity, low-frequency-noise behavior and detectivity were reported relative to simpler structures in the LWIR branch.

**Role:** functional blocking-contact benchmark only.

## S-R7-05 — Smith, Musca, Faraone 2000 — 2-D blocking-contact model

**Class:** Primary-A / Same-RP-01-team  
**Citation:** E. P. G. Smith, C. A. Musca, L. Faraone, “Two-dimensional modelling of HgCdTe photoconductive detectors,” *Infrared Physics & Technology* 41(3), 175–186 (2000).  
**DOI:** `10.1016/S1350-4495(99)00054-7`

**Key modeled practical-device parameters:** `n+≈1×10^16 cm^-3`, depth `≈3 µm` were used in a model fit.

**Restriction:** model parameters, not direct RP-01 RIE measurements.

**Role:** demonstrates that contact depth/doping/geometry change 2-D field and excess-minority-carrier distributions; supports detector-level sweepout qualification.

## S-R7-06 — Ashley & Elliott 1982 — accumulation contact predecessor

**Class:** Primary-A / historical predecessor  
**Citation:** T. Ashley, C. T. Elliott, “Accumulation effects at contacts to n-type cadmium-mercury-telluride photoconductors,” *Infrared Physics* 22(6), 367–376 (1982).

**Direct abstract-level findings:**

- contact deposition preceded by ion cleaning;
- resulting accumulation behavior supported by several photoconductive measurements;
- small-device responsivity increased by about 5×;
- response time strongly field dependent;
- approximate contact recombination velocities `~200–500 cm/s` for samples including x=0.30 and x=0.21.

**Role:** historical functional benchmark for low contact recombination velocity; not RP-01 fabrication process.

## S-R7-08 — Smith et al. 1984 — HgCdTe heterojunction contact photoconductor

**Class:** Primary-A / historical predecessor  
**Citation:** D. L. Smith, D. K. Arch, R. A. Wood, M. Walter Scott, “HgCdTe heterojunction contact photoconductor,” *Applied Physics Letters* 45, 83–85 (1984).

**Key result:** a wider-bandgap HgCdTe alloy layer is inserted between metal and the normal-bandgap photoconductor. Theory and fabricated devices show strong sweepout suppression; reported 80-K responsivity ~4.5×10^5 V/W at 30 V/cm and >1.5×10^6 V/W at 125 V/cm.

**Restriction:** epitaxial/isotype heterojunction architecture, not RIE n+/n.

**Class:** Primary-A / historical predecessor  
**Citation:** D. K. Arch, R. A. Wood, D. L. Smith, “High responsivity HgCdTe heterojunction photoconductor,” *Journal of Applied Physics* 58(6), 2360–2370 (1985).  
**DOI:** `10.1063/1.335959`

**Role:** principal detailed follow-on for the wider-bandgap isotype heterojunction blocking-contact architecture.

## S-R7-10 — D. L. Smith 1984 — blocking contact noise/responsivity theory

**Class:** Primary-A / theory  
**Citation:** D. L. Smith, “Effects of blocking contacts on generation-recombination noise and responsivity in intrinsic photoconductors,” *Journal of Applied Physics* 56(6), 1663–1669 (1984).  
**DOI:** `10.1063/1.334155`

**Key physical result:** increased contact blocking raises responsivity and can move D* toward background-limited behavior, but also causes responsivity and g-r noise to roll off at lower frequency. Blocking-contact optimization therefore trades responsivity/noise against bandwidth.

**Procedures informed:** P08F, P08G, P13.

## Round-7 central distinction

`rho_c` from TLM and minority-carrier contact recombination velocity `S_c` are not the same quantity.

A contact can be a good majority-carrier ohmic contact while still being a poor minority-carrier recombination boundary, or vice versa. RP-01 local qualification must therefore combine TLM with sweepout/responsivity/noise/temporal behavior.
