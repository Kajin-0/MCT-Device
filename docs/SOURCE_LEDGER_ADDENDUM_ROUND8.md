# Source ledger addendum — source-recovery round 8

**Date:** 2026-08-15 America/New_York

This addendum records the upstream Te-rich source-preparation, finite-melt and composition-matched orientation sources promoted during round 8.

## S-R8-01 — Bowers–Schmit US4317689A re-audit: well/inventory boundary

**Class:** Primary-B / same-Honeywell lineage  
**Citation:** J. E. Bowers, J. L. Schmit, U.S. Patent 4,317,689, “Mercury containment for liquid phase growth of mercury cadmium telluride from tellurium-rich solution.”

**Direct apparatus details re-confirmed:**

- graphite slider with one or more through-wells for Te-rich growth solution;
- wells shown tapered, smaller at the bottom;
- plug caps the growth-solution well;
- substrate recess and corresponding slider recess sized generally to substrate dimensions;
- separate shallow HgTe/HgTe+Te source well and interconnected Hg-distribution grooves/moats;
- N2 purge before heating;
- H2 flow;
- growth near 500 °C after above-liquidus equilibration and below-liquidus growth;
- example growth duration ~0.5 h.

**Critical negative result:** full patent text does not state growth-well diameter, depth, volume, liquid depth, growth-charge mass, gram quantity or source-use count.

Historical melt inventory therefore remains `OPEN` and apparatus-specific.

## S-R8-02 — Sanz-Maudes et al. 1990 — finite-liquid / Hg-loss model

**Class:** Primary-A / process physics  
**Citation:** J. Sanz-Maudes, J. Sangrador, T. Rodriguez, et al., “Numerical simulation of the growth of HgCdTe layers by liquid phase epitaxy from Te-rich solutions: The effect of liquid dimensions and mercury loss,” *Journal of Crystal Growth* 106, 303–317 (1990).  
**DOI:** `10.1016/0022-0248(90)90076-W`

**Key direct abstract-level results:**

- finite liquid dimensions explicitly included;
- moving solid/liquid interface and Hg loss modeled;
- step cooling, ramp cooling and supercooling considered;
- for growth times longer than a characteristic time related to liquid thickness, growth rate decreases;
- Hg loss drives the grown layer Cd-richer;
- outcome depends on liquid dimensions and Hg-loss flux;
- model can reproduce experimental behavior when those system parameters are known.

**Role:** primary physical basis for P03D. Melt inventory/depth cannot be released independently of growth time and Hg-loss state.

## S-R8-03 — Radhakrishnan, Sitharaman, Gupta 2003 — explicit source synthesis/mass example

**Class:** Primary-A / different composition branch  
**Citation:** J. K. Radhakrishnan, S. Sitharaman, S. C. Gupta, “Liquid phase epitaxial growth of HgCdTe using a modified horizontal slider,” *Journal of Crystal Growth* 252, 79–86 (2003).  
**DOI:** `10.1016/S0022-0248(02)02530-7`

**Direct experimental details:**

- typical source composition around z≈0.049, y≈0.84, generally x≈0.20 branch;
- 10 g growth compound synthesized from 6N Cd, Te and Hg;
- evacuated quartz ampoule synthesis at 700 °C for 8 h;
- synthesized charge ground and thoroughly mixed;
- ~4.8 g used per single growth run;
- 3 g HgTe used for Hg-loss compensation;
- high-purity/high-density graphite slider;
- 15×15×1 mm CdZnTe substrate recess.

**Restriction:** these masses/thermal synthesis conditions are not combined with the Honeywell xL=.082/yL=.810 tie line and must not be presented as the historical x≈0.30 recipe.

**Role:** candidate source-preparation/melt-inventory methodology for P03C/P03D.

## S-R8-04 — in-situ Te-solution preparation alternative

**Class:** Primary-A / different process branch  
**Citation:** “Slider LPE growth of MCT using in situ Te-solution preparation,” *Journal of Crystal Growth* 87, 365–371 (1988).  
**DOI:** `10.1016/0022-0248(88)90189-3`

**Role:** demonstrates that Te-rich source can be prepared in situ by Hg-vapor transport into a Cd-rich Te melt, avoiding separate ampoule preparation/homogenization.

**Restriction:** separate process family; not historical Honeywell source synthesis.

## S-R8-05 — Suh et al. 1992 — composition-matched Hg-loss-control branch

**Class:** Primary-A / independent horizontal-slider x≈0.30 branch  
**Citation:** S. H. Suh, S. W. Moon, J. S. Kim, S. W. Lim, N. J. Kwak, H. K. Kim, J. M. Kim, “Slider liquid-phase epitaxial growth of Hg0.8Cd0.2Te, Hg0.7Cd0.3Te and Hg0.3Cd0.7Te with precise control of alloy compositions,” *Journal of Crystal Growth* 121(3), 417–422 (1992).  
**DOI:** `10.1016/0022-0248(92)90152-9`

**Direct institutional-record findings:**

- slider LPE used for x=0.2, 0.3 and 0.7;
- process designed so solution composition remains constant during growth;
- graphite button between solution and HgTe wells reported very effective in controlling Hg loss;
- composition uniformity/reproducibility, morphology and electrical properties reported.

**Role:** independent composition-matched evidence that Hg-loss-control geometry materially affects x/reproducibility.

**Restriction:** do not transplant graphite-button geometry into Honeywell P03 without defining a separate apparatus branch.

## S-R8-06 — Kwak/Lim/Choi/Kim/Suh 1991 — x≈0.30 (111)Cd misorientation study

**Class:** Primary experimental / independent x≈0.30 slider branch  
**Citation:** N. J. Kwak, S. W. Lim, I. H. Choi, J. M. Kim, S. H. Suh, “Effect of misorientation of (111) CdTe substrate on the surface morphology of Hg0.7Cd0.3Te grown by an LPE process,” *Journal of the Korean Institute of Metals* 29(1), 15–22 (1991). KIST institutional record URI available; UCI `I410-ECN-0102-2008-580-001848490`.

**Direct abstract-level findings:**

- Te-rich slider LPE of Hg0.7Cd0.3Te;
- CdTe substrates of `(111)Cd` orientation;
- varying misorientation magnitude/direction;
- at 1° miscut, characteristic terrace fronts perpendicular to misorientation direction;
- terrace width decreases and height increases as miscut rises;
- above about 2°, terrace structure begins transforming toward wave-like morphology;
- step bunching invoked for initial growth; dislocations become important as terraces reach ~10–30 µm width.

**Role:** composition-matched support for a P07B low-degree miscut screen around the `{111}` Cd-terminated family.

**Restriction:** CdTe rather than CdZnTe and independent process; not proof of RP-01/Honeywell face.

## S-R8-07 — independent x≈0.30 growth-time morphology study

**Class:** Primary experimental / independent x≈0.30 slider branch  
**Citation:** S. W. Lim, I. H. Choi, J. M. Kim, W. J. Song, S. W. Moon, S. H. Suh, “Evolution of surface morphology during LPE growth of Hg0.7Cd0.3Te,” *Journal of the Korean Institute of Metals* 28(2), 185–190 (1990).

**Direct abstract-level findings:**

- slider LPE on (111) CdTe;
- surface evolves from wave-like in early growth toward terrace-like with increasing growth time;
- terrace width increases with growth time;
- IR transmission used to determine composition.

**Role:** reinforces P03B/P07B coupling between growth time and morphology for x≈0.30.

## S-R8-08 — Schmit & Bowers 1979 Te-rich x=.40 precursor

**Class:** Primary-A / same-Honeywell lineage  
**Citation:** J. L. Schmit, J. E. Bowers, “LPE growth of Hg0.60Cd0.40Te from Te-rich solution,” *Applied Physics Letters* 35, 457–458 (1979).

**Direct abstract-level findings:**

- atmospheric-pressure slider LPE;
- composition uniform to ±0.01 mole fraction except a ~3-µm interdiffusion region;
- as-grown p-type ~1×10^17 cm^-3;
- annealable to n-type ~4×10^15 cm^-3.

**Role:** same-lineage precursor showing strong Te-rich composition uniformity but does not close source-synthesis route.

## Round-8 central conclusions

1. Honeywell x≈0.30 **composition is historically anchored**, but source synthesis, total melt mass and well volume remain undisclosed.
2. Small melt volume is an advantage of Te-rich slider LPE, but finite-liquid depletion can still matter depending on liquid depth and growth time.
3. Hg-loss control is a composition-control variable, not merely containment.
4. A composition-matched independent x≈0.30 branch supports `{111}` Cd-oriented, low-degree miscut screening, but does not close RP-01 polarity.
