## S24-01 — Jones et al. 1982

C. L. Jones, M. J. T. Quelch, P. Capper, J. J. G. Gosney, “Effects of annealing on the electrical properties of CdxHg1−xTe,” *Journal of Applied Physics* 53, 9080–9092 (1982), DOI `10.1063/1.330419`.

**Evidence class:** primary journal / direct anneal-state evidence.

Material:
- x approximately 0.17–0.31.

Direct architecture distinction:
- closed-tube and open-tube annealing;
- Hg vapor controlled with a mercury reservoir;
- isothermal: reservoir T approximately equal to HgCdTe T;
- two-temperature: reservoir T lower than HgCdTe T.

Direct physical conclusion:
- isothermal Hg-rich conditions can convert native-defect p-type material to n-type;
- two-temperature closed-tube conditions can convert n to p and control acceptor concentration with sample temperature/Hg pressure;
- in open-tube two-temperature experiments, Hg pressure affected equilibration kinetics even where final acceptor concentration was less sensitive.

**Use in P31:** establishes `T_s` and `T_Hg/pHg` as independent process coordinates.

**Not recovered:** full apparatus dimensions, reservoir mass, exact run-by-run temperatures/times from accessible text.

## S24-02 — Harman US4642142

T. C. Harman, US4642142, “Process for making mercury cadmium telluride.”

**Evidence class:** primary patent / same Te-rich LPE process family.

Direct anneal anchors:
- post-growth in-situ pseudo-isothermal Hg-saturated anneal architecture;
- post-growth pseudo-two-zone architecture;
- typical pseudo-isothermal point approximately 250 °C for 1 h;
- then cooling to room temperature;
- broad 200–300 °C anneal region and controlled Hg pressure are part of the disclosed process framework.

**Use:** strongest primary process-family center for a first low-temperature RP-01 transfer screen.

**Restriction:** reported electrical endpoints are not identical to RP-01; do not promote 250 °C/1 h to a production recipe.

## S24-03 — Nagahama et al. 1984

K. Nagahama, R. Ohkata, K. Nishitani, T. Murotani, “LPE growth of Hg1−xCdxTe using conventional slider boat and effects of annealing on properties of the epilayers” (1984).

**Evidence class:** primary LPE/anneal journal/proceedings evidence, near-composition.

Direct accessible findings:
- Te-rich LPE HgCdTe x≈0.17–0.30;
- as-grown p-type;
- Hg-overpressure anneals 250–400 °C;
- 250–300 °C produced well-behaved n-type layers without apparent composition change;
- 400 °C produced detectable compositional change near the interface.

**Use:** constrains first RP-01 transfer to sub-300 °C unless a separate high-temperature purpose exists.

**Not recovered:** exact reservoir geometry, Hg inventory, times and cooldown.

## S24-04 — Schaake/Tregilgas US4481044

H. F. Schaake, J. H. Tregilgas, US4481044, “High-temperature Hg anneal for HgCdTe.”

**Evidence class:** primary patent / Texas Instruments defect-control lineage.

Direct process anchors:
- conventional low-temperature post anneal below about 300 °C in an ampoule containing liquid Hg;
- typically at least 1 h and often much longer;
- high-temperature saturated-Hg branch around 600 °C for ~4 h or more for dislocation/Te-precipitate control;
- demonstrated x range includes ~0.2–0.3;
- sealed quartz tube with enough Hg to provide saturated Hg atmosphere.

**Use:** confirms sealed-ampoule elemental-Hg architecture and that low-temperature anneal time depends strongly on prior material state.

**Restriction:** high-temperature 600 °C treatment is not automatically required for RP-01 LPE material.

## S24-05 — Schaake et al. 1985

H. F. Schaake, J. H. Tregilgas, J. D. Beck, M. A. Kinch, B. E. Gnade, “The effect of low temperature annealing on defects, impurities, and electrical properties of (Hg,Cd)Te,” *J. Vac. Sci. Technol. A* 3, 143–149 (1985), DOI `10.1116/1.573186`.

**Evidence class:** primary journal kinetics/defect evidence.

Accessible direct conclusions:
- low-temperature Hg annealing drives Hg in-diffusion;
- Te precipitate annihilation can cause substantial dislocation multiplication for some starting states;
- long-time front depth scales approximately with square root of anneal time and inversely with square root of excess-Te/vacancy-related starting condition;
- rapidly diffusing impurities can redistribute/getter.

**Use:** prevents treating carrier conversion as the only release output; defect/precipitate state is part of P31.

## S24-06 — US5079192 two-zone sealed-ampoule architecture

J. H. Tregilgas et al., US5079192, “Method of preventing dislocation multiplication of bulk HgCdTe and LPE films during low temperature anneal in Hg vapor.”

**Evidence class:** primary patent / directly relevant LPE HgCdTe furnace architecture.

Direct preferred hardware/process example:
- long sealed ampoule;
- LPE HgCdTe at one end;
- sufficient elemental Hg for saturated atmosphere at remote end;
- two-zone furnace independently heats sample and reservoir;
- sample about 400 °C first-stage;
- Hg reservoir initially below about 200 °C;
- ~1 h first-stage example;
- reservoir then raised to ~390 °C with sample ~400 °C;
- ~1 h second stage;
- low-temperature stoichiometric anneal below 325 °C for several hours to several days;
- cool to room temperature.

Direct design warning:
- reservoir kept cooler than sample to prevent Hg deposition on/dissolution of specimen.

**Use:** concrete two-zone geometry and cooldown/source-coupling logic.

**Restriction:** this is a dislocation-control sequence, not an RP-01 low-temperature recipe.

## S24-07 — US5028296 multi-step supersaturation control

J. H. Tregilgas, US5028296, “Annealing method.”

**Evidence class:** primary patent.

Direct process examples:
- Hg-rich high-temperature anneal followed by intermediate and low-temperature anneals;
- bulk example: ~270 °C for 3–7 days after prior high-T conditioning;
- ~400 °C intermediate anneal 1–2 h for near-surface vacancy supersaturation reduction in one branch;
- longer times for thick slices;
- alternate incremental/continuous cooling paths.

**Use:** proves that thermal path and starting thickness/history strongly alter required low-T duration.

**Restriction:** bulk slice durations are not transferable to 9.5-µm LPE epilayers.

## S24-08 — Chandra/Schaake/Kinch 2003

D. Satish Chandra, H. F. Schaake, M. A. Kinch, “Low-temperature annealing of (Hg,Cd)Te,” *Journal of Electronic Materials* 32, 810–815 (2003), DOI `10.1007/s11664-003-0075-5`.

**Evidence class:** primary journal kinetics.

Direct accessible result:
- annealing rate depends strongly on vacancy concentration, x and T;
- rate decreases with increasing CdTe mole fraction over the studied range;
- behavior resembles composition-dependent activated diffusion.

**Use:** blocks direct time transfer from x≈0.20 to x≈0.30.

---

## S24-09 — Archer/Palfrey 1991 mercury diffusion

N. Archer, H. Palfrey, “Mercury diffusion in Hg1−xCdxTe” (1991).

**Evidence class:** primary radiotracer diffusion study.

Direct accessible anchors:
- Hg-203 reservoirs used as vapor diffusion sources;
- closed-tube isothermal anneals 300–400 °C;
- bulk and LPE material studied;
- at 310 °C under saturated Hg, reported near-surface diffusion coefficient scale ~`1.4×10^-13 cm²/s`;
- at 400 °C bulk saturated-Hg data show two apparent diffusion components.

**Use:** supports thickness- and temperature-dependent kinetics; not used to derive a production dwell for RP-01 because x/state and mechanism differ.

## S24-10 — later saturated-Hg pressure example

US20120264254A1 reports for a different HgCdTe device branch:
- pure-Hg saturated environment around 250 °C corresponds to substantial Hg vapor pressure (~0.3 atm in its cited curve);
- 370 °C can approach ~1.3 atm;
- sealed/pressurized containment is therefore a real engineering issue.

**Evidence class:** later primary patent / transfer engineering evidence.

**Use:** reinforces that pHg cannot be treated as negligible and that pressure-rated containment/EH&S must be separately engineered.

**Restriction:** do not use those pressure numbers as the P31 calibration relation without checking the source relation and actual apparatus.

# Round-24 evidence conclusions

1. `T_s`, `T_Hg`, and enclosure/source geometry must be retained separately.
2. Isothermal and two-temperature anneals are physically different process branches.
3. A literature `250 °C / 1 h` point is a valid first screening anchor, not an RP-01 recipe.
4. Near-x≈0.30 evidence strongly favors first screening below 300 °C if the objective is n-type stoichiometry control without composition shift.
5. Ampoule geometry/source inventory are not closed by saying “Hg saturated.”
6. Cooldown is causal and must retain both sample/source traces.
7. Defect/precipitate/dislocation response must be measured alongside Hall state.
8. Bulk multi-day anneal times must not be copied onto thin LPE epilayers.
