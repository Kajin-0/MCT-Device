# Round 64 - same-lineage RIE, passivation and late-stage full-text closure

**Date:** 2026-08-17 America/New_York  
**State:** documentary evidence strengthened; no new physical HgCdTe experiment.

## Purpose

Round 64 integrates fifteen newly recovered full papers against the Round-63 controlled evidence state. The acquisition was targeted at the remaining high-value upstream Honeywell slider-LPE literature, same-UWA RIE/LBIC process physics, photoconductor passivation/noise coupling, and HgCdTe/metal interface chemistry.

Round 64 does **not** convert analogous numbers into historical RP-01 facts. Evidence classes remain `RP`, `SL`, `PT`, `DER`, `SYN`, and `OPEN`.

## 1. Honeywell horizontal-slider LPE is now better constrained

### Schmit, Hager and Wood 1982 - PT, Honeywell upstream lineage

The full paper confirms a Honeywell atmospheric-pressure horizontal-slider process using:

- high-purity graphite slider hardware;
- horizontal quartz tube;
- flowing high-purity atmospheric-pressure H2;
- external regulated furnace;
- push-rod actuation;
- previously compounded/homogenized Te-rich Hg-Cd-Te solution;
- polished CdTe substrates, 1 mm thick and typically 1 x 1 cm or 2 x 3 cm, (111).

The sequence is explicit: heat the solution above liquidus (typically near 500 C), lower below liquidus, slide the supercooled solution over the substrate, grow isothermally under the selected supercooling, then slide the solution away after the chosen time.

Reported process behavior includes:

- Te-rich liquid with y approximately 0.8;
- usual growth times approximately 1-20 min for layers up to about 30 um;
- thickness control extending to about 50 um through supercooling/time;
- initial growth rates of several um/min, decreasing strongly after roughly 15 min;
- eight sequential layers with mean x approximately 0.212 and run-to-run standard deviation approximately 0.004;
- within-wafer x variation on a 1 x 1 cm layer no greater than approximately +/-0.001;
- terracing with amplitudes below roughly 0.4 um peak-to-trough in the reported examples;
- occasional 1-10 um pinholes at low defect density.

**Consequence:** this is strong PT confirmation of the Honeywell process family and its supercooling/time control philosophy. It does not close the historical RP-01/Fermionics boat dimensions, gas flow, well geometry, clearance, actuator speed, or exact growth recipe.

## 2. Semiclosed low-x slider evidence strengthens source-use and equilibration controls

### Nemirovsky et al. 1982 - PT

For x approximately 0.2-0.3 material, the paper describes a semiclosed slider with:

- approximately 4.5 g HgTe reservoir;
- graphite cover spanning the solution and HgTe reservoir;
- a groove communicating Hg vapor between them;
- approximately 3.5 g growth solution;
- reservoir replacement every run;
- reuse of the same growth solution for several, approximately five, runs;
- approximately 20% HgTe-reservoir mass loss during a typical 2-3 h cycle near 460 C, while the growth solution practically retained its weight.

A useful process branch reports:

- equilibration around 463 C;
- approximately 5 C supercooling;
- growth from about 458 to 455 C;
- cooling rate about 0.25 C/min;
- approximately 10 um layer in 15 min.

The same work shows that substrates can change during the equilibration interval even before melt contact: crystallites were observed on covered substrates exposed to the vapor environment. It also reports a pre-load surface branch using 0.3 um Al2O3 polishing followed by 10% Br2/methanol for 10-15 s immediately before loading.

**Consequence:** P30/P03 must treat `source-use number`, Hg-reservoir depletion, substrate vapor exposure during equilibration, and deliberate melt-back as first-class coordinates. These remain PT, not RP-01 historical assignments.

## 3. Same-UWA Plasma Technology RIE physical states are now substantially recovered

### Smith et al. 1999 - SL

For x approximately 0.31 HgCdTe, the full paper reports:

- Plasma Technology parallel-plate reactor;
- water-cooled cathode at 16 C;
- CH4/5H2 process gas notation;
- total flow 30 sccm;
- 400 mTorr;
- 0.4 W/cm2 RF power density;
- printed dc bias 180 V;
- RIE rate about 0.15 um/min.

The associated LBIC system used 1.047 um CW excitation, about 400 mW/cm2, approximately 3 um spot and 2.5 um scan step. A sealed-Hg anneal at 200 C for 17 h restored the RIE-modified p-type material in the reported branch.

This is the strongest same-lineage reactor-state branch yet recovered, but it remains distinct from the RP-01 `100 mTorr / 50 W / 64 sccm / 60 s` blocking-contact exposure. Therefore RP-01 self-bias, electrode geometry, RF frequency, wafer loading and actual sample temperature remain OPEN.

## 4. The previously loose approximately 8 um conversion-depth result is now tied to an explicit SL process state

### Musca et al. 1998 - SL

The full paper reports an x approximately 0.31 Fermionics n-type sample, initially about 20 um thick and thinned to about 10 um, exposed through a nominal 350 x 350 um window in a Plasma Technology parallel-plate reactor:

- cathode 18 C;
- H2 27 sccm;
- CH4 5 sccm;
- 430 mTorr;
- 0.4 W/cm2;
- approximately 200 V dc bias;
- 30 s exposure;
- physical material removal only about 0.075 um.

LBIC plus sequential depth removal nevertheless showed an electrically modified n+/n junction signature extending to approximately 8 um in that branch.

This result must be preserved alongside, not averaged with, the much shallower conversion depths reported in other UWA branches. Electrical conversion depth is highly sensitive to material state, plasma state, mask state and measurement method.

**New state:** `SL-DEPTH-BRANCH-CLOSED / RP01-CONVERSION-DEPTH-OPEN`.

## 5. Photoresist thickness/profile becomes a first-class RIE coordinate

Musca et al. 1998 further show that an apparent long lateral doping gradient can arise because photoresist becomes progressively thinner near a mask edge. Plasma-induced electrical modification can occur beneath sufficiently thin resist even when the region is nominally masked.

Round 64 therefore requires, for RIE conversion/depth qualification:

- resist material and process ID;
- measured resist thickness over protected regions;
- edge-profile/taper measurement around the RIE opening;
- minimum resist thickness above the relevant conversion boundary;
- post-RIE resist state;
- correlation of LBIC lateral extent with the measured mask profile.

A drawn mask opening alone is not a sufficient physical boundary condition.

## 6. RIE conversion is electrically multilayered

### Antoszewski et al. 2000 - SL

For x approximately 0.30 LPE HgCdTe, a short parallel-plate RIE branch used approximately:

- 100 W;
- 40 sccm total;
- 5:1 H2/CH4;
- 500 mTorr;
- 2 min.

The electrically converted depth was reported as 2.0 +/- 0.5 um. QMSA at 77 K separated a high-mobility converted bulk-electron population from a lower-mobility surface-electron population.

### Nguyen et al. 2002 - SL

A related whole-surface branch used:

- CH4/H2 ratio 1:3;
- 0.4 W/cm2;
- 2 min;
- about 0.3 um physical material removal.

Field-dependent differential Hall and QMSA again separate a thin damaged/degenerate surface-electron layer from a deeper high-mobility n-type region. In x approximately 0.31 samples the electrically converted depth for this short branch was generally below approximately 1 um.

**Consequence:** one-carrier Hall values after RIE cannot by themselves establish conversion depth or the useful blocking-contact state. P05/P34 interpretation must permit at least a surface-electron population and a deeper converted-bulk population and use field-dependent Hall/QMSA or an equivalent resolved method when the distinction matters.

## 7. RIE conversion mechanism is state-history dependent

### White et al. 2001 - SL

Using x approximately 0.31 Fermionics LPE material and deuterium as a SIMS tracer, the paper supports multiple hydrogen-related populations rather than one simple vacancy-annihilation picture. The reported SIMS-process branch used approximately:

- D2 54 sccm;
- CH4 18 sccm;
- 100 mTorr;
- 0.2 W/cm2;
- about 200 V dc bias;
- cooled stage 15 C.

The authors explicitly estimate rather than directly measure the actual HgCdTe temperature; they considered it unlikely to exceed about 80 C. Subsequent 80 C and 100 C bakes redistributed mobile hydrogen.

**Consequence:** preserve the difference between chuck/cathode temperature and actual sample temperature, and record post-RIE thermal history before interpreting Hall/LBIC/contact behavior.

## 8. Mercury annealing can reverse an SL RIE conversion branch

### Smith et al. 1998 - SL

For x approximately 0.31 extrinsically doped p-type HgCdTe, RIE at 400 mTorr, CH4/H2 and 90 W created p-to-n conversion detectable by LBIC. A sealed-tube mercury anneal at 200 C for 17 h removed the converted region in that experiment, with Hall measurements returning to approximately the initial p-type electrical state.

**Consequence:** RIE state and Hg-anneal state are coupled and can be reversible in at least one same-UWA branch. Do not use `200 C / 17 h` as an automatic RP-01 repair step.

## 9. Mesa RIE can catastrophically degrade photoconductor performance

### Smith et al. 2000 - SL

This study used Fermionics n-type LPE x approximately 0.31 material and fabricated photoconductors with wet-etched and plasma-etched mesas on the same wafer.

The paper literally prints the plasma-mixture notation `H2/5CH4` for the reported 70-min branch, with:

- total flow 30 sccm;
- 400 mTorr;
- 0.4 W/cm2;
- water-cooled cathode 16 C;
- 80 V dc bias;
- 70 min;
- about 0.15 um/min physical etch rate.

The literal gas notation is retained because it differs from several related same-UWA papers; Round 64 does not silently normalize it.

At 80 K, 10 V/cm and 60 degree FOV, wet and dry devices had the same approximately 4.7 um cutoff, but the plasma-mesa device had roughly three times lower responsivity. Effective minority-carrier lifetime was approximately 10.5 us for the wet device and 3.7 us for the plasma device. At about 2 kHz, reported noise was approximately 6.5 nV/sqrt(Hz) for the wet device and about 50 nV/sqrt(Hz) for the plasma device, with excess 1/f behavior persisting beyond 10 kHz in the dry-processed device. Reported D* was approximately 2.5e11 versus 1.0e10 cm Hz^1/2/W for wet versus dry mesa processing in that experiment.

**Consequence:** expanding RIE area/dose beyond localized blocking-contact conversion requires a downstream detector-level gate including responsivity, effective lifetime, noise spectrum and D*. Similar DC resistance is not evidence of equivalent detector quality.

## 10. LBIC depth must be calibrated, not inferred from one lobe spacing

### Musca et al. 1999 - SL measurement lineage

Known-junction-depth calibration structures at 2, 4 and 6 um showed systematic LBIC response changes, but modeling demonstrates sensitivity to:

- junction doping;
- illumination wavelength;
- frontside/backside illumination;
- device geometry;
- minority-carrier diffusion length.

**Consequence:** use calibrated witness structures, a validated forward model, destructive cross-calibration, or another independent depth technique before reporting an absolute electrical-conversion depth from LBIC alone.

## 11. Photoconductor passivation is a coupled electrical state, not an oxide-thickness target

### Pal et al. 1999 - PT

A gated n-HgCdTe photoconductor test structure demonstrates that positive surface accumulation can simultaneously:

1. lower surface recombination and increase effective minority-carrier lifetime;
2. create a surface shunt that lowers detector resistance and can reduce responsivity.

Their structure used 300 A anodic oxide plus 2700 A ZnS beneath a gate, and the experiment extracted shunt resistance and effective lifetime while sweeping the surface state. The reported best-fit interface-trap density was of order 5-7e11 cm^-2 eV^-1 for that device.

**Consequence:** P25/P12/P13 should treat `{Qss, Dit, Rsh,surface, tau_eff, responsivity}` as a coupled acceptance vector. Oxide thickness alone is not a sufficient passivation endpoint.

## 12. Anodic-oxide surface charge has optical/electrical memory

### Schoolar et al. 1982 - PT, x=0.30

On anodized n-type Hg0.7Cd0.3Te, the paper reports positive fixed charge of order 5e11 cm^-2 and shows that visible illumination above roughly 2.2 eV can neutralize oxide/interface charge by internal photoemission into oxide traps. Recovery in darkness occurs on timescales from minutes at room temperature to weeks at 77 K. Applied electric field can also redistribute charge and alter surface potential.

The reported optical oxide gap is about 3.4 eV.

**Consequence:** an anodic-oxide/MCT electrical measurement is not uniquely defined by temperature and oxide thickness. Record at minimum:

- prior visible/UV exposure;
- dark-rest interval;
- prior applied gate/bias field;
- time since field removal;
- measurement temperature;
- C-V or equivalent interface-state preconditioning sequence.

`Qss` is a state variable with history, not a permanent scalar material constant.

## 13. Surface shunts can contribute directly to photoconductor noise

### Bhan et al. 2004 - PT

Measurements/modeling of n-HgCdTe photoconductor arrays required an additional noise contribution associated with surface shunt resistance from accumulation layers at anodic-oxide interfaces. A bulk generation-recombination-only description did not reproduce the measurements.

The example device/model branch used x approximately 0.214, 77-78 K, 20 kHz measurement frequency and a fitted surface potential around 69 mV corresponding to Qss approximately 1.2e12 cm^-2. These values are apparatus/device-specific PT examples.

**Consequence:** when detector resistance or noise behavior indicates surface shunting, P12 must include a passivation/shunt branch rather than forcing all excess noise into bulk g-r or 1/f categories. Passivation affects resistance, responsivity, lifetime and noise simultaneously.

## 14. Au deposition can chemically/electronically restructure HgCdTe interfaces

### Davis et al. 1984 - PT

On UHV-cleaved p-Hg0.72Cd0.28Te(110), evaporated Au caused strong Te redistribution/out-diffusion and large changes in near-surface cation/anion ratio. Initial Au deposition also shifted band bending by about 0.2 eV toward flat-band/accumulation. The experiment used a tungsten evaporation basket, quartz-oscillator thickness monitoring and operating pressure near 1e-9 Torr.

**Consequence:** metal/HgCdTe interface chemistry is a process state. P26 should continue to record base pressure, source, deposition rate/trajectory, QCM geometry, sample thermal history and time between semiconductor exposure and metallization. This paper does **not** reproduce RP-01 Cr/Au deposited onto an RIE-converted n+ region and therefore provides no direct RP setpoint.

## 15. Nemirovsky and Bahir 1989 is a cross-check, not a setpoint-promoting source

The review corroborates the practical anodic-oxide family already supported by primary papers: room-temperature KOH/ethylene-glycol/water anodization, n-HgCdTe accumulation, fixed positive charge around 5e11-1e12 cm^-2, and use of native anodic oxide in photoconductors. Because Round 64 has the primary sources, the review is retained as `Secondary-A` context and does not promote new hard setpoints.

## 17. Critical non-promotions

Round 64 rejects the following tempting inferences:

1. Schmit/Hager/Wood 1982 does not provide the RP-01/Fermionics machine drawing.
2. Nemirovsky's 4.5-g HgTe reservoir and 3.5-g solution are not Honeywell/RP-01 inventories.
3. Smith-1999 180-V dc bias is not the RP-01 blocking-contact self-bias.
4. Musca-1998 approximately 8-um electrical conversion depth is not automatically the RP-01 contact-conversion depth.
5. The very different 0.5-8 um electrical conversion depths in UWA papers must not be averaged into a synthetic universal depth.
6. Smith-2000's literal `H2/5CH4` notation must not be silently rewritten to match other UWA papers.
7. White-2001's 15 C stage temperature is not a direct HgCdTe sample-temperature measurement.
8. Smith-1998 `200 C / 17 h` is not a universal post-RIE repair recipe.
9. Pal/Bhan surface-charge and shunt values are not RP-01 passivation acceptance limits.
10. Schoolar's illumination-memory result means Qss cannot be treated as static without preconditioning history.
11. Davis's Au-on-cleaved-p-HgCdTe interface is not equivalent to RP-01 Cr/Au on an RIE-converted n+ contact region.

After Round 64, broad paper mining has still lower expected value than archival/apparatus recovery. Highest-value remaining records are:

2. electrode diameters/areas, spacing, matching-network configuration, RF frequency and measured dc self-bias for that RP-01 run;
3. actual sample-temperature measurement or thermal calibration for the RP-01 exposure;
4. Fermionics/Honeywell slider machine drawings and x approximately 0.30 production travelers;
5. original anodization-cell drawing/electrode spacing/area ratio for the RP-01 lineage;
6. RP-01 Cr/Au evaporator/QCM/source-to-sample geometry;
7. original cryostat/package/readout implementation.

The correct response to these remaining gaps is archival recovery or local qualification, not analogical numerical filling.
